Top Stories

1. Ethan Allen Files Its Proxy, Frames DGB as a Governance Risk

Ethan Allen (NYSE: ETD) filed its definitive proxy statement on September 24 ahead of a November 4 shareholder meeting, urging votes for CEO Farooq Kathwari's slate and against DGB Investment's nominees. The company conceded revenue sits below its 2006 peak but attributed roughly two-thirds of the fiscal 2026 decline to State Department, China and dealer revenue drops, not core retail weakness. It touted a five-year total shareholder return of 49.1% against a peer median of negative 42.7%, debt-free status and gross margin above 59%. For retailers watching the fight, the outcome will decide who runs the CEO search and whether Ethan Allen's manufacturing and logistics consolidation strategy continues or gets rewritten by an activist board.

Source: Furniture Today

2. Massachusetts Retailer Eddie's Furniture Closes After 91 Years

Eddie's Furniture Co. in Holyoke, Massachusetts is closing after 91 years, with owner Job Hicks citing rising prices, lack of federal funding and traffic that had nearly vanished since last summer. All inventory is being sold at 25% off, and Hicks will keep the doors open until it sells, after which he plans to sell the property, which realtors suggest could become an expanded parking lot. The business began as a horse barn in 1870 before Hicks' father started selling furniture in 1935. It is a stark reminder that independent retailers without pricing power or fresh traffic drivers are running out of runway, and that a strong local legacy no longer guarantees survival through a soft consumer stretch.

Source: Furniture Today

3. Rowe Expands Allteriors With Proprietary Outdoor Cushion

Rowe Furniture is expanding its Allteriors outdoor line, launched last April, with new textiles, tailored slipcovers and a proprietary down-alternative cushion built with quick-draining reticulated foam and antimicrobial fiber. President Bobby Robinson said the goal was extending Rowe's indoor silhouettes and comfort outdoors rather than building a separate outdoor category. The library now totals 47 outdoor performance fabrics, including 21 new textures sourced partly from Italy, and popular indoor pieces like Adler and Rothko are getting outdoor debuts. New introductions ship by the end of 2026 for Spring 2027. Retailers get a chance to sell outdoor as a design-forward extension of an already-trusted indoor brand rather than a discount seasonal category.

Source: Furniture Today

4. UE Living Debuts Anti-Pinch Motion Technology at High Point

UE Living is introducing SensoDrive, a motion upholstery technology with intelligent anti-pinch detection that pauses reclining motion when it senses an obstruction, at High Point Market. The feature will become standard across UE Living's modern, casual and transitional motion products at multiple price points, alongside whisper-quiet operation and double-tap reclining. General Manager Ron Yu said safety and comfort had to work together in the design. The showroom, in Furniture Plaza space 410, opens October 16. As motion upholstery becomes a larger share of upholstery sales, safety features that used to be premium extras are becoming baseline expectations, giving retailers a concrete talking point against private-label imports lacking the same engineering.

Source: Furniture Today

5. A Sales Rep's Take: Furniture's Problem Is the Handoff, Not Conversion

Furniture Today columnist Mike Root argues that the industry's conversion debate misses the real issue: by the time a shopper walks into a store, they have often already researched the product online, on social media or with AI, and the store visit is validation, not discovery. The failure point, he writes, is the handoff from marketing and technology to the sales floor, when an undertrained RSA cannot demonstrate a product a customer already knows about. Root, who trains associates on Cozzia massage chairs, says salespeople need enough confidence in a few key features, not mastery of every spec. The lesson for retailers: invest as seriously in floor training as in the marketing spend that drives traffic to the door.

Source: Furniture Today

  • Retailers Tighten AI Oversight but Agentic Sprawl Keeps Growing - A Netskope report finds ungoverned AI tool use among retail employees fell from 70% to 44% over the past year as more companies centralize tool approval, but 90% of employees still use AI trained on customer data. Retail-sector AI agents connecting to remote MCP servers jumped 400% during the survey period, and 56% of AI-related data violations Netskope tracked involved regulated customer data. For furniture retailers rolling out AI chat, design or inventory tools, the message is to pair adoption with visibility, since agentic tools are multiplying faster than most IT teams can track them.

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • JCPenney's Furniture Sales Jump 41% Even as Total Revenue Falls - JCPenney's overall net sales fell 8% to $1.3 billion in Q2, dragged down by apparel, but furniture led home category growth with a 41% increase versus last year. Gross margin rose to 39.2% from 38.7% despite higher product costs, and the retailer launched JCPenney Marketplace, adding third-party products to its website, with early results outperforming expectations. Net income fell 51% to $52 million. Furniture is proving to be a bright spot even inside a struggling department store, a signal that category demand exists for retailers willing to merchandise and price it well.

With depending-on-the-source estimates putting online transactions at 30% to more than 40% of home furnishings sales, independents whose e-commerce share sits in the single digits should treat agentic checkout as the next front door, not a future problem, and start asking vendors whether their product data is even structured for AI discovery.

  • Stitch Fix Warns Active Client Growth Won't Return in 2027 - Stitch Fix closed fiscal 2026 with net revenue up 6.4% to $1.3 billion but active clients down 1.4% year over year to just under 2.3 million, and executives told analysts they do not expect client growth to return in fiscal 2027. Net revenue per active client rose nearly 8% to $592, and the net loss narrowed 56% to $12.6 million. CEO Matt Baer said a tougher consumer environment is driving the caution. For subscription and personalization-driven home furnishings models, the lesson is that improving unit economics does not fix a shrinking customer base, and acquisition costs deserve as much scrutiny as retention.
    Source: Retail Dive

The One Thing

Audit the Handoff From Ad to Associate

Furniture Today's Mike Root makes the case that most lost sales happen after the shopper walks in already informed, not before. This week, sit in on three floor conversations and check whether the associate knows what the customer already researched online. If the handoff from marketing to the floor is weak, no amount of ad spend or AI shopping tool will fix the close.
