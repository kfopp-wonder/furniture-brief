Thursday, September 17, 2026

Good morning. The Commerce Department finally handed the category a green number, and vendors are responding by loading up on ready-to-ship inventory rather than waiting for the recovery to prove itself. John Thomas is expanding Quick Ship by more than 50 pieces, Streamline Art is bringing 350 new products to October Market and Liberty Furniture adds a 41-year sales veteran to push its whole-home line. Meanwhile Pinterest and Shipt are racing to put AI directly between browsing and buying, a reminder that the online share of this category keeps climbing whether an independent store is ready or not.

Top Stories

1. John Thomas Bulks Up Quick Ship With 50 New Pieces

John Thomas Furniture, the finished-furniture division of Whitewood Industries, is adding more than 50 new products to its Quick Ship program, all stocked in its North Carolina warehouse and shippable immediately starting mid-September. The additions also flow into the company's mixed direct container program, giving retailers more flexibility to build assortments. VP Sales and Development Bryan Sprinkles says the move answers the question shoppers are asking most in this economy: can they actually get the piece they want, now. The expansion, including five new dining collections, will show at October High Point Market. For retailers, in-stock solid wood removes the biggest objection in a cautious market: the wait.

Source: Home News Now

2. Furniture Sales Post First Gain Since October

The Commerce Department's advance estimates show furniture and home furnishings store sales hit an adjusted $11.398 billion in August, up 1.9% year over year and 0.9% over July, the category's first increase since October 2025. Year-to-date sales still trail 2025's pace by 1.6%, though August trimmed that gap by a tenth of a point. Broader retail rose 6% year over year, with building material and garden dealers up 5.1%. One month does not make a trend, but it is the first data point in nearly a year that gives retailers something other than a defensive story to tell at the bank and to the sales floor.

Source: Furniture Today

3. Streamline Art Brings 350 New Products to October Market

Streamline Art will launch over 350 new products at October High Point Market, headlined by its new Woven Studio collection, 60 pieces of watercolor imagery printed on domestically manufactured linen canvas in 14 sizes and three floating-frame finishes. The company is also adding 250 pieces to its hand-embellished art program, leaning into biophilic and moody palettes, and expanding its grand-scale sizing up to 30 by 70 inches. Wall decor remains one of the easiest margin categories to refresh without disrupting a floor plan. Retailers building vignettes around textured, tactile trends now have a domestic-sourced option to point to on the tag.

Source: Home News Now

4. Liberty Furniture Hires 41-Year Veteran as Sales SVP

Liberty Furniture named Bryan Edwards senior vice president of sales, pulling him from Bernhardt, where he ran residential upholstery and export sales for seven years, with earlier stops at A.R.T. Furniture, Aspenhome, Mirador, Lea, American Drew, Flexsteel and Broyhill. He will report to President Jonathan Cowles and lead a group of independent reps across the U.S. as Liberty expands its whole-home line. A hire this senior signals a vendor betting on volume growth through its rep network rather than direct accounts alone. Retailers working with Liberty can expect a more aggressive push on new collections heading into High Point.

Source: Home News Now

5. Culp Executives to Brief Investors on Turnaround Progress

Culp Inc. (NASDAQ: CULP) said President and CEO Iv Culp and CFO Mary Beth Hunsberger will present at the Water Tower Research Virtual Insights Conference on September 23, discussing business strategy, fiscal 2027 trends and the company's global sourcing platform. Culp described the appearance as a chance to highlight steps taken to strengthen its platform and improve profitability. As a major mattress-fabric and sewn-cover supplier, Culp's commentary on demand and margin trends is a useful early read on where bedding manufacturers stand heading into the fall selling season. Retailers and reps in bedding should watch for signals on cost and capacity.

Source: BedTimes Magazine

Market Pulse

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • Pinterest Lets Shoppers Restyle Their Own Room With AI - Pinterest is launching Restyle in beta across the U.S. and Canada, an AI tool built on Pinterest Intelligence that lets users photograph their own room and then add, swap or erase furniture, art and paint colors, including items pulled from their saved boards. The feature aims to close the gap between inspiration and purchase. For furniture sellers whose online share still lags the 30% to 40% range seen elsewhere in home furnishings, a tool that visualizes their own SKUs in a customer's actual room is a low-cost way to shorten the path from browsing to a floor visit or checkout.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Shipt Launches AI Cart Builder to Match Instacart - Target-owned Shipt rolled out Ask Shipt, an AI assistant that lets customers describe what they need or upload a photo to build a shopping cart, launching the same day rival Instacart introduced its own assistant, Clementine. Shipt executive Katie Stratton says cart-building is the hardest part of the shopping journey for most customers. The pattern of AI collapsing search into checkout is spreading fast across retail, and furniture sites without a comparable list-building or visual-search tool are asking shoppers to do more manual work than competitors already require.
    Source: Modern Retail
  • Pinterest Pitches Advertisers on a Full-Funnel Platform - Pinterest unveiled new ad tools including Visual Search Ads and an expanded Performance+ suite with manual A/B testing, telling advertisers the platform now drives purchases, not just inspiration. CEO Bill Ready cited 640 million monthly users and more than 80 billion monthly searches, over half tied to buying intent and 96% unbranded. For furniture and home brands, unbranded high-intent search volume at that scale is exactly the traffic an underdeveloped e-commerce operation cannot capture without a presence there, and paid clicks to advertisers have grown five times over three years.
    Source: Modern Retail

Supply Chain & Trade

  • CBP Sets October Start for Bigger Tariff Refund Wave - Customs and Border Protection will begin processing refunds October 6 for finally liquidated entries tied to now-invalidated IEEPA tariffs, provided businesses submitted a valid importer of record number by the end of July, according to a court filing. This phase covers roughly $11.4 billion, or 6.9% of IEEPA tariffs collected. As of September 11, CAPE has accepted $134.7 billion in refunds with $122 billion sent to Treasury for disbursement. Importers of furniture components should confirm their filing status now and read eligibility rules closely before booking any refund into cash flow projections.
    Source: Supply Chain Dive

AI & Tech Watch

  • AI Agents Learn to Make Phone Calls for You - Instinct and Meta's Muse have both added calling features that let AI agents book reservations, join cancellation lists or resolve billing problems on a user's behalf, with Instinct reportedly nearing a $10 billion valuation. The concierge functions turn a chat assistant into something closer to a personal service line. For furniture retailers, this points toward a near future where a shopper's AI agent could call the store to check stock or schedule delivery, which puts a premium on having accurate, quickly retrievable inventory and appointment data on the other end of that call.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Watching AI Agents Now Requires More AI - As companies deploy AI agents for longer and more complex tasks, oversight has become its own crisis, illustrated by a Hugging Face incident where nearly 12,000 agents coordinated faster than humans could track. The emerging fix is putting a second AI in the loop to monitor the first, though critics like blogger Simon Willison warn a malicious agent could try to trick its monitor. Retailers piloting AI for pricing, customer service or inventory decisions should build in human checkpoints now, before agent-driven tools scale past the point anyone can review their output line by line.
    Source: TechCrunch

The One Thing

Put Your Own Room Visualizer to Work

Pinterest's Restyle and Shipt's cart-building assistant both show where shopping is headed: AI that turns browsing into a finished cart inside someone else's room, not a showroom. An independent retailer still under 10% online share should pick one AI visualization or list-building tool this quarter and load it with actual SKUs and floor inventory, so the endless aisle points back to product that can ship, not just inspire.
