Thursday, September 17, 2026

Good morning. The Commerce Department says home furnishings stores swung back to positive territory in August, and the industry's response is telling: John Thomas is expanding Quick Ship by more than 50 SKUs, Cozey is opening a permanent Montreal store, and Liberty Furniture just hired a 41-year veteran to shore up its sales bench. Add Pinterest's new room-restyling AI and Shipt's cart-building assistant, and the thread is clear. Retailers are being asked to prove availability, trust and speed all at once, not just style.

Top Stories

1. John Thomas Bets On Availability Over Waitlists

John Thomas Furniture, the solid-wood division of Whitewood Industries, is adding more than 50 new products to its Quick Ship program, all stocked in its North Carolina warehouse and available for immediate shipment starting mid-September. The additions also flow through the company's mixed direct container program, giving retailers a second way to build assortments quickly. VP Sales and Development Bryan Sprinkles framed it as answering shopper hesitation directly: is it well made, worth the price, and can I get it now. The expanded program debuts at October High Point Market alongside five new dining collections.

Source: Home News Now

2. DOC Data Shows Furniture Stores Turned Positive in August

Commerce Department figures show home furnishings stores moved back into positive territory in August, a reversal worth watching after months of soft comparisons tied to tariff uncertainty and a sluggish housing market. The category has been one of the more volatile reads in monthly retail sales data this year. For retailers, one positive month is not a trend, but it is the first data point in a while that supports cautious optimism heading into fourth quarter planning and inventory commitments for spring.

Source: Home Textiles Today

3. Liberty Furniture Hires 41-Year Veteran to Lead Sales

Liberty Furniture named Bryan Edwards senior vice president of sales, pulling him from Bernhardt where he ran residential upholstery and export sales for seven years. Edwards previously held leadership sales roles at A.R.T. Furniture, Aspenhome, Mirador, Lea and American Drew, and started at Broyhill in 1985. He will manage Liberty's independent rep network and report to President Jonathan Cowles as the company adds collections across upholstery and case goods. For retailers, a veteran hire signals Liberty is investing in the relationship layer at a moment when vendors that can be trusted to deliver matter more than ever.

Source: Home News Now

4. Cozey Bets On Brick and Mortar in Montreal

DTC furniture retailer Cozey is opening its first permanent Montreal store, extending a physical expansion beyond its online roots. The move follows a broader pattern among digitally native furniture brands adding showroom footprints to let shoppers test fit, fabric and scale before buying. For traditional retailers, it is a reminder that the online-only advantage has shrunk, and that DTC brands now compete on the same real estate and service terms as legacy stores.

Source: retail-insider.com

5. Streamline Art Leans Into Texture With Woven Studio

Streamline Art will launch its Woven Studio collection at October High Point Market, printing watercolor imagery on linen canvas across 60 pieces in fourteen sizes, framed in vintage-style floating frames in black, champagne and French white. The domestically manufactured line joins more than 250 new hand-embellished art pieces built around biophilic and moody color palettes, plus an expansion of grand-scale sizing up to 30 by 70 inches. For retailers building out wall decor assortments, the linen texture and larger formats target the tactile, curated look designers keep asking for.

Source: Home News Now

Market Pulse

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • Shipt Joins Instacart in the AI Cart-Building Race - Shipt launched Ask Shipt, an AI assistant that lets customers describe what they want or upload a photo to auto-build a cart, the same day Instacart rolled out its own assistant, Clementine. Shipt's version also surfaces curated bundles and lets brands bid for placement through its retail media network. For furniture retailers watching marketplace behavior, the pattern points toward AI increasingly owning the list-building step, meaning product data quality determines who gets recommended first.
    Source: Modern Retail
  • Pinterest Lets Shoppers Restyle Their Own Rooms - Pinterest is launching Restyle in beta for the U.S. and Canada, letting users photograph their room and prompt AI to swap in furniture, art, paint colors or entire style themes like bohemian or industrial. The tool runs on Pinterest Intelligence, built with Nvidia hardware and open source models. For furniture and decor sellers, the feature shortens the path from saved pin to purchase, and makes clean, well-tagged product imagery a direct input into whether a shopper's saved item gets tried in their actual space.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Toys R Us Adds 120 Stores Before the Holidays - Toys R Us is opening 120 new stand-alone stores this holiday season with partner Go Retail Group, bringing its footprint to 160 locations, some with influencer content studios, candy shops and cafes. The expansion follows a smaller version last year and continues a mall and off-mall physical push from a brand that once liquidated entirely. For furniture retailers, it is another data point that physical retail investment is accelerating even among brands built on cautionary bankruptcy tales, when the format and experience are right.
    Source: Retail Dive

Supply Chain & Trade

  • CBP Opens Next Phase of Tariff Refund Processing - Customs and Border Protection will begin processing refunds Oct. 6 for finally liquidated entries tied to now-defunct IEEPA tariffs, covering importers who submitted a valid importer of record number by the end of July. The phase addresses roughly $11.4 billion of the $134.7 billion in refunds CAPE has accepted so far. Importers who filed after July 30 must wait for further instructions, and the Justice Department's pending appeal means litigation status still affects who qualifies. Retailers with import exposure should confirm filing dates and eligibility with customs counsel before assuming a refund is coming.
    Source: Supply Chain Dive

AI & Tech Watch

  • AI Assistants Race to Add Phone Calls - Instinct and Meta's Muse both added calling features this week, letting agents phone businesses to book reservations, join waitlists or resolve billing issues on a user's behalf. Instinct is reportedly in talks to raise at a $10 billion valuation. For furniture retailers, agents that can call stores directly mean phone scripts, hold times and staff answers are becoming part of the customer experience even when no human initiated the call.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Monitoring AI Agents Now Requires More AI - As companies deploy larger fleets of AI agents, oversight has become its own problem, illustrated by an incident where nearly 12,000 agents coordinated faster than humans could track. The emerging fix is using AI to monitor AI, though researchers warn a malicious agent could learn to deceive its monitor. For retailers experimenting with agentic tools in customer service or inventory, the lesson is to build in human checkpoints now, before agent volume outpaces anyone's ability to review it.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Tiny AI Models Aim to Run on Any Device - Startup PrismML released Bonsai 2 27B, a compressed version of an open source model that shrinks to 5.9 GB, small enough for a PC or high-end smartphone, while matching 98% of the original's benchmark performance. The company raised a $22.25 million seed round and counts Databricks co-founder Ion Stoica as an adviser. For furniture retailers, smaller on-device models could eventually power in-store visualization or design tools without relying on constant cloud connectivity, lowering the cost of deploying AI on the sales floor.
    Source: TechCrunch

The One Thing

Audit What You Can Actually Ship Now

Pull the top 20 SKUs driving floor traffic and check real lead times against what sales associates are quoting. With Quick Ship programs expanding and consumers asking harder questions before they buy, a gap between promise and delivery is where sales are lost. Fix the quote before the fall market brings in new product to compound the problem.
