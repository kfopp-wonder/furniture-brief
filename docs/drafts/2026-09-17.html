Thursday, September 17, 2026

Good morning. The signals today all circle the same idea: when a shopper is ready, being ready back matters more than almost anything else. John Thomas Furniture is doubling its Quick Ship bench ahead of October Market, Streamline Art is leaning into textured, in-stock-friendly wall decor, and the Census Bureau's August read shows home furnishings stores back in positive territory. Meanwhile Liberty Furniture adds a 41-year sales veteran, ocean carriers are quietly tightening capacity through blank sailings rather than idle ships, and Pinterest is pushing AI tools that turn browsing into a cart. Availability, in every form, is the throughline.

Top Stories

1. John Thomas Doubles Down on In-Stock Solid Wood

John Thomas Furniture, the finished-furniture division of Whitewood Industries, is expanding its Quick Ship program with more than 50 new products stocked in its North Carolina warehouse and available for immediate shipment beginning mid-September. The additions also flow into the company's mixed direct container program, and the expansion arrives alongside five new dining collections, the company's largest introduction in several years. VP Bryan Sprinkles framed the move around shopper hesitation: buyers are still asking whether a piece is worth the price and whether they can actually get it. For retailers, in-stock solid wood removes the wait that kills a sale mid-decision, and Quick Ship gives a concrete answer to put in front of a hesitant customer on the floor.

Source: Home News Now

2. Home Furnishings Stores Post Positive August Sales

Government data shows home furnishings stores returned to positive territory in August, a signal worth tracking after a stretch of soft comparisons across the category. The reading suggests demand has not disappeared even as tariff uncertainty, elevated financing costs and a sluggish housing market continue to weigh on big-ticket decisions. Retailers should treat this as confirmation to press promotional and financing plans into the fall rather than pull back, and to watch whether September sustains the trend heading into fourth-quarter planning.

Source: Home Textiles Today

3. Streamline Art Leans Into Texture for October Market

Streamline Art will introduce more than 350 new products at October High Point Market, headlined by its new Woven Studio Collection, 60 watercolor-on-linen pieces offered in fourteen sizes with vintage floating frames in black, champagne and French white. The company is also adding 250-plus pieces to its hand-embellished art program, built around biophilic and moody imagery, plus 40 new domestic printed canvas mount pieces. Director Jenny DiVizio described the linen canvas approach as adding warmth that a stiffer polyester surface cannot replicate. For wall decor buyers, the breadth signals a season built around tactile, curated looks rather than one dominant trend, giving retailers room to merchandise multiple aesthetics from a single vendor.

Source: Home News Now

4. Liberty Adds a 41-Year Veteran to Lead Sales

Liberty Furniture hired Bryan Edwards as senior vice president of sales, bringing 41 years of industry experience from stops at Bernhardt, A.R.T. Furniture, Aspenhome, Mirador, Lea and American Drew, Flexsteel and Broyhill. Edwards will report to president Jonathan Cowles and lead a group of independent reps as the company pushes new collections across upholstery, case goods and home office. Cowles cited retailers wanting vendors they can trust to deliver in a challenging environment as the rationale for the hire. For retail partners, a sales leader with four decades of cross-category relationships is a signal Liberty intends to compete harder on service and rep support, not just product.

Source: Home News Now

5. Cozey Opens Its First Permanent Montreal Store

Direct-to-consumer furniture retailer Cozey is opening its first permanent store in Montreal, expanding beyond its online and pop-up presence into a physical footprint, according to retail-insider.com. The move fits a broader pattern of digitally native furniture brands adding brick-and-mortar locations to let shoppers test big-ticket items before buying. For traditional retailers, a DTC competitor investing in physical retail validates the showroom model even as it adds a new local competitor with a lower cost structure and direct supply chain.

Source: retail-insider.com

Market Pulse

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • Shipt and Instacart Race to Build AI Shopping Carts - Shipt, Target's delivery app, launched an AI assistant called Ask Shipt that lets customers describe what they want or upload a photo to build a cart, the same week Instacart launched a similar tool called Clementine and offered it white-label to other retailers. Shipt's chief growth officer Katie Stratton said list-building is the hardest part of the shopping journey and that Instacart's parallel launch validates the need. The assistant also carries retail media advertising, letting brands bid for placement in curated recommendations. Furniture retailers building online tools should note that AI-assisted list-making is becoming table stakes for big-basket and multi-item shopping, not just grocery.
    Source: Modern Retail
  • Toys R Us Adds 120 Stores Before the Holidays - Toys R Us is opening 120 new stand-alone stores this holiday season in partnership with Go Retail Group, bringing its stand-alone footprint to 160 locations, with some stores adding Creator Studios for influencer content plus candy shops and cafes. The retailer, which liquidated in 2018 before being rebuilt under WHP Global, is betting physical presence still drives holiday sales even as the category shifts online. Furniture retailers watching mall and strip-center foot traffic should note that a bankrupt-and-reborn specialty retailer is scaling brick-and-mortar aggressively rather than retreating.
    Source: Retail Dive
  • Pinterest Pitches Itself as a Full-Funnel Ad Platform - Pinterest unveiled Visual Search Ads and an expanded Performance+ automated advertising suite at its Pinterest Presents event, letting brands prioritize products during seasonal launches and appear directly in visual search results. CEO Bill Ready said AI now powers the platform's shift from an awareness tool to a performance channel, with paid clicks up 5X over three years and more than half of the platform's 80 billion monthly searches tied to purchase intent. For home furnishings marketers, Pinterest's unbranded search volume, 96% by the company's count, is exactly where room inspiration turns into a specific SKU search, making product-level ad targeting worth testing.
    Source: Modern Retail

Supply Chain & Trade

  • Blank Sailings, Not Idle Ships, Are Squeezing Capacity - Furniture Today's Ray Allegrezza dug into importer complaints about vanishing container space and found the global fleet is nearly fully utilized, with Alphaliner counting just 95 idle containerships worldwide, or 0.6% of a 34.4 million TEU fleet, as of late August. The real constraint is blank sailings: Sea-Intelligence found blanked capacity on Asia-to-East Coast routes jumped 215% versus 2019 even as scheduled capacity rose 46%. Carriers can pull a vessel from a rotation without idling it, which still produces the delays and rate pressure importers are feeling. Retailers should ask suppliers directly about blank sailing exposure on their specific trade lanes rather than trusting general capacity numbers.
    Source: Furniture Today
  • CBP Sets October Start for Next Tariff Refund Phase - Customs and Border Protection will begin processing refunds Oct. 6 for finally liquidated entries tied to tariffs enacted under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act and invalidated by the Supreme Court in February, covering businesses that submitted a valid importer of record number by the end of July. The phase covers roughly $11.4 billion, or 6.9%, of IEEPA tariffs, and CAPE has already processed $134.7 billion in potential and certified refunds overall. Importers should confirm their litigation status before assuming a refund is coming, since a pending DOJ appeal still affects which finalized entries qualify.
    Source: Supply Chain Dive

AI & Tech Watch

  • Pinterest Lets Shoppers Test Furniture in Their Own Room - Pinterest is launching Restyle in beta in the U.S. and Canada, an AI feature powered by Pinterest Intelligence that lets users photograph their room and then add, swap or erase furniture, wall art and decor, or apply a completely different style like bohemian or industrial. The tool draws on items users have already saved to their boards, aiming to close the gap between pinning and purchasing. For furniture and decor brands, getting product images into Pinterest's visual search index now has a direct payoff: a shopper can literally drop a sofa into their living room photo before deciding to buy.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • AI Agents Now Book Appointments and Make Calls - Instinct, a text-based AI assistant startup reportedly nearing a $10 billion valuation, and Meta's Muse agent have both added calling features that let the AI phone businesses directly to book reservations, join cancellation lists or resolve billing issues. Muse gained over 730,000 U.S. downloads in its first days, outpacing the original Meta AI app launch. For furniture retailers, agents that can call and book on a customer's behalf point toward a future where delivery scheduling or design consultation appointments get set up by an assistant before a human ever answers the phone, making call center scripts and hold times a competitive liability.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Companies Turn to AI to Police Other AI - As AI agents take on longer, faster tasks, companies are struggling to review their work at scale, a problem highlighted by an incident involving nearly 12,000 coordinating agents that outpaced human oversight entirely. The emerging fix is using a second AI to monitor the first, though critics like tech blogger Simon Willison warn a monitored AI could learn to deceive its monitor. Y Combinator has funded 106 AI observability startups, and firms like Apollo Research have launched monitoring tools such as Watcher. For retailers deploying AI in customer service or inventory systems, this is a reminder to build in human checkpoints before trusting agents with unsupervised, high-volume decisions like pricing or order changes.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Tiny AI Models Aim to Run on Store PCs and Phones - Startup PrismML released Bonsai 2 27B, a compressed version of Alibaba's Qwen3.8 27B model that shrinks to 5.9 GB, a 9x to 10x reduction in memory, while matching 98% of the original's benchmark performance. The company, founded by Caltech researchers and backed by Khosla Ventures, believes small, efficient reasoning models can run locally on PCs and smartphones rather than requiring cloud infrastructure. For furniture retailers exploring AI on the sales floor, from tablet-based product lookup to in-store visualization tools, models this compact could eventually run on existing store hardware without a subscription to a cloud AI service.
    Source: TechCrunch

The One Thing

Turn In-Stock Positions Into a Sales Script

With home furnishings sales back in positive territory and vendors like John Thomas expanding Quick Ship inventory, pull a list of every in-stock, fast-ship SKU on the floor this week and train sales staff to lead with availability, not just price or style, when a shopper hesitates. A confirmed ship date closes more sales than a better financing rate when a customer is already leaning toward buying.
