Thursday, September 17, 2026

Good morning. August brought home furnishings stores back into positive territory, and the vendor community is acting like it believes the recovery. John Thomas is stocking more than 50 new pieces for immediate shipment, Streamline Art is loading up October High Point with 350 new products, and Liberty Furniture just hired a 41-year sales veteran to steer growth. Meanwhile Pinterest is pushing AI further into the path to purchase, and CBP is finally opening the door to tariff refunds. The thread connecting all of it: speed, whether in fulfillment, product development or getting cash back from Washington, is becoming the differentiator.

Top Stories

1. John Thomas Bets Big on Ready-to-Ship Solid Wood

John Thomas Furniture, the finished-furniture division of Whitewood Industries, is expanding its Quick Ship program with more than 50 new products stocked in its North Carolina warehouse and ready to ship starting mid-September. The additions also flow through the company's mixed direct container program, and the expansion will headline the brand's presence at the fall High Point Market. VP Sales and Development Bryan Sprinkles framed it as answering shopper hesitation directly: quality, value and availability. In a market where tariff uncertainty and cautious spending are stretching decision cycles, inventory sitting on the ground is a closing tool. Retailers weighing new solid-wood vendors have a fresh reason to ask every supplier how fast they can actually deliver.

Source: Home News Now

2. Home Furnishings Stores Post Positive August

The Department of Commerce's retail sales data shows home furnishings stores returned to positive territory in August, per Home Textiles Today. The category has spent much of the past two years in decline as high rates and a slow housing market kept big-ticket purchases on hold, so a positive print is a meaningful signal even before the details are fully known. For retailers and vendors alike, it is the clearest sign yet that pent-up demand may be starting to release. Store operators should watch whether the improvement holds through September traffic and whether it is broad-based or concentrated in promotional categories.

Source: Home Textiles Today

3. Liberty Furniture Hires 41-Year Veteran Bryan Edwards

Liberty Furniture named Bryan Edwards senior vice president of sales, bringing 41 years of industry experience including stints at Bernhardt, A.R.T. Furniture, Aspenhome, Mirador, Lea and American Drew, and Flexsteel. He reports to President Jonathan Cowles and will manage a network of independent reps to grow sales nationally as Liberty expands its whole-home offering. Edwards called Liberty's upholstery and case goods breadth a fit for his career specialties and praised the company's value story and finish trends. The hire signals Liberty is stocking up on sales leadership ahead of a heavier new-collection push, a move retailers should watch when planning fall showroom visits at Centers for High Point.

Source: Home News Now

4. Streamline Art Loads October Market With 350 New Pieces

Streamline Art will introduce more than 350 new products at October High Point Market, headlined by its new Woven Studio collection, 60 domestically manufactured pieces of watercolor imagery printed on linen canvas in fourteen sizes up to 38 by 60 inches. The company is also adding more than 250 pieces to its hand-embellished art program with new biophilic and moody imagery categories, plus 40 new domestic printed canvas mount pieces. President Mark Wiltshire said the expansion responds directly to retailer requests for larger-scale pieces to fit bigger rooms and design projects. Wall decor buyers should treat the linen-canvas texture and grand-scale sizing as a fast way to refresh art walls without a full assortment overhaul.

Source: Home News Now

5. Pinterest Adds AI Room Redesign Tool Called Restyle

Pinterest is launching Restyle in beta in the U.S. and Canada, an AI feature powered by Pinterest Intelligence that lets users photograph their room and then prompt the AI to add furniture, art or decor from their saved Pins, swap items in and out, or reimagine the whole space in a new style. The tool builds on Pinterest's Nvidia-powered Pinterest Intelligence platform and aims to close the gap between inspiration boards and actual purchases. For furniture retailers, a visualization tool sitting inside the platform where so much home shopping starts raises the bar on product photography and tagging. Retailers should check whether their SKUs are pinnable and well-labeled before this rolls out more broadly next month.

Source: TechCrunch

Market Pulse

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • Shipt Launches AI Assistant to Build Shopping Carts - Target-owned Shipt launched Ask Shipt, an AI assistant that lets customers describe what they need or upload a photo to build a cart, the same week Instacart launched a similar tool called Clementine. Shipt's Katie Stratton said cart-building is the hardest part of the shopping journey, and the assistant can recommend curated bundles with advertising tie-ins through Shipt Media. Furniture retailers running their own e-commerce should note that natural-language cart-building is becoming table stakes, and product data needs to answer descriptive, unstructured questions, not just category filters.
    Source: Modern Retail
  • Toys R Us Adds 120 Stores for the Holidays - Toys R Us is opening 120 new stand-alone stores this holiday season in partnership with Go Retail Group, bringing its footprint to 160 locations, some featuring influencer content studios, candy shops and cafes. The rapid physical expansion from a brand that liquidated in 2018 shows specialty retail still sees a case for stores when the format is right-sized and experiential. Furniture retailers weighing pop-up or small-format showrooms ahead of holiday traffic can take the cue that speed to open, not just square footage, is driving these bets.
    Source: Retail Dive
  • Cozey Opens First Permanent Montreal Store - DTC furniture retailer Cozey is opening its first permanent store in Montreal, expanding its physical footprint, according to retail-insider.com. The move continues a pattern of digitally native furniture brands adding brick-and-mortar to support conversion and returns. Traditional retailers competing with Cozey in Canadian markets should watch which categories the store leads with and whether it changes local price competition.
    Source: retail-insider.com

Supply Chain & Trade

  • CBP Opens Next Phase of Tariff Refund Processing - U.S. Customs and Border Protection will begin processing refunds October 6 for finally liquidated entries tied to now-invalidated IEEPA tariffs, provided businesses submitted a valid importer of record number by the end of July. The phase covers roughly $11.4 billion, or 6.9%, of IEEPA tariffs, part of a total $134.7 billion in refunds CAPE has accepted as of September 11, with $122 billion already sent to Treasury. Trade advisors caution that litigation status still affects eligibility since the Justice Department has appealed the underlying refund order. Importers of furniture components should confirm their IOR filings are current and consult counsel before assuming a refund is coming.
    Source: Supply Chain Dive

AI & Tech Watch

  • Pinterest Rolls Out Visual Search Ads for Brands - Pinterest unveiled Visual Search Ads and an expanded Performance+ suite with manual A/B testing, letting brands prioritize products during seasonal moments. CEO Bill Ready said AI now powers a platform built for full-funnel conversion, not just inspiration, with 640 million monthly users and 80 billion searches a month, 96% of them unbranded. Furniture and decor brands sitting on Pinterest boards have new tools to convert browsing into checkout, and the unbranded search volume is an opening for smaller manufacturers without big ad budgets to get discovered on style and category terms.
    Source: Modern Retail
  • AI Agents From Meta and Instinct Learn to Make Calls - Meta's Muse and startup Instinct both added calling features that let AI agents phone businesses to book appointments or resolve account issues, with Instinct reportedly nearing a $10 billion valuation. The capability turns AI assistants into a concierge layer between consumers and retailers, which means furniture stores may start fielding calls placed by a customer's AI rather than the customer directly. Retailers should brief call center and sales staff on how to verify and serve an AI-initiated inquiry without losing the personal touch that closes big-ticket sales.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Startups Use AI to Police Other AI Agents - As companies deploy AI agents for longer, more complex tasks, oversight has become a bottleneck, with incidents like nearly 12,000 coordinating agents outpacing human review. The fix gaining traction is putting a second AI in the loop to monitor the first, though skeptics warn a malicious agent could learn to deceive its monitor. Retailers experimenting with agentic tools for pricing, inventory or customer service should build in human checkpoints now, before automation scales past the point anyone can audit it by hand.
    Source: TechCrunch

The One Thing

Ask Every Vendor Their Ship Date

With home furnishings sales back in positive territory and John Thomas leaning hard into Quick Ship, retailers should audit their top vendors this week on true in-stock timing, not catalog promises. Ask which SKUs are warehouse-ready versus container-only, and push for the same speed commitment from every supplier competing for floor space heading into the fall market.
