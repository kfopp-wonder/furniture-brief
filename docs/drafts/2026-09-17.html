Thursday, September 17, 2026

Good morning. The Census Bureau's August retail data give home furnishings stores their first genuinely good headline in a while, and vendors are moving to capitalize before shoppers change their minds. John Thomas is stocking more than 50 new Quick Ship pieces, American Leather is opening a premium upholstery tier, and Streamline Art is bringing 350 new products to October Market. Liberty Furniture adds a 41-year sales veteran to close deals with retailers who want proof a vendor can deliver. Underneath it all, CBP starts processing IEEPA refunds next month, a reminder that tariff turbulence is still working its way through the books.

Top Stories

1. Home Furnishings Stores Post a Positive August

The Census Bureau's retail sales data show home furnishings and home furnishings stores back in positive territory in August, according to Home Textiles Today. The reading follows months of soft or negative comparisons across the category as tariff uncertainty and a sluggish housing market weighed on big-ticket spending. A single month does not make a trend, but it gives retailers heading into fall market a data point to bring to lenders and landlords. Store leaders should watch whether September holds the gain before recalibrating fourth-quarter open-to-buy plans.

Source: Home Textiles Today

2. John Thomas Bets on Speed With Bigger Quick Ship

John Thomas Furniture, the finished-furniture division of Whitewood Industries, is adding more than 50 new products to its Quick Ship program, all warehoused in North Carolina and shippable immediately starting mid-September. The additions also flow into the company's mixed direct container program, giving retailers flexibility on assortment building. VP Bryan Sprinkles framed the move around a shopper who is still buying but wants proof of value and availability before committing. For retailers, in-stock solid wood at approachable price points is a hedge against lost sales when customers will not wait months for delivery.

Source: Home News Now

3. American Leather Opens a Designer-Focused Upholstery Tier

American Leather is launching A.L. Studio, a made-to-order premium upholstery collection featuring sculptural sofas, eight accent chairs, two beds, a daybed and a chaise, backed by more than 500 upholstery options plus COM and COL. Executive Vice President Dan Campbell said the line is meant to show designers the company's speed-and-quality manufacturing can support more original silhouettes beyond its namesake sleeper business. The collection debuts at October High Point and reaches consumers through designers and select luxury retailers starting spring 2027. Retailers with design-trade relationships should get ahead of the spring rollout now.

Source: Furniture Today

4. Streamline Art Adds 350 Pieces and a New Linen Line

Streamline Art will debut its Woven Studio Collection at October High Point, 60 domestically made pieces of watercolor imagery printed on linen canvas in fourteen sizes up to 38 by 60 inches, framed in a vintage floating style. The launch is part of a broader introduction of more than 350 new products, including 250-plus hand-embellished pieces built around biophilic and moody color trends. President Mark Wiltshire said the expansion answers retailer requests for larger scale art to fit bigger rooms and projects. Wall decor remains a low-risk way to refresh a floor between big furniture resets.

Source: Home News Now

5. Liberty Furniture Hires a 41-Year Sales Veteran

Liberty Furniture named Bryan Edwards senior vice president of sales, pulling him from Bernhardt, where he ran residential upholstery and export sales for seven years, with earlier stints at A.R.T. Furniture, Aspenhome, Mirador, Lea, American Drew and Flexsteel. He reports to President Jonathan Cowles and will lead the company's independent rep network across the U.S. Cowles said retail partners are looking for vendors they can trust to deliver in a challenging environment. A veteran hire at this level signals Liberty is prioritizing account continuity and delivery reliability as it expands its whole-home offering.

Source: Home News Now

Market Pulse

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • Shipt Follows Instacart Into AI Cart Building - Target-owned Shipt launched Ask Shipt, an AI assistant that turns a shopper's description or a photo into a cart-ready list, the same week Instacart rolled out its own assistant, Clementine. Chief Growth and Strategy Officer Katie Stratton told Modern Retail that list-making and cart-building are the hardest parts of the shopping journey, and that Shipt's retail media network can surface sponsored items inside AI recommendations. Furniture retailers building their own site search or chat tools should note the pattern: assistants that reduce the effort of building a cart, not just search, are becoming the default expectation.
    Source: Modern Retail
  • Pinterest Lets Shoppers Restyle Their Own Rooms - Pinterest is launching Restyle in beta for the U.S. and Canada, an AI tool built on Pinterest Intelligence that lets users photograph a room and then swap in furniture, wall art, lighting or paint colors, including items pinned from Pinterest boards. Users can also ask for a full style change, such as bohemian or industrial. The feature aims to close the gap between inspiration and purchase, a persistent problem for furniture retailers whose product gets pinned but never bought. Retailers with strong Pinterest catalogs should check whether their images are tagged well enough to surface inside Restyle results.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Toys R Us Bets on Physical Stores for Holiday - Toys R Us is opening 120 new stand-alone stores this holiday season with partner Go Retail Group, bringing its footprint to 160 locations, some featuring influencer content studios, candy shops and cafes. The expansion follows a smaller push of 30 stores last year and comes alongside shop-in-shops at Macy's and airports. For furniture retailers, it is a reminder that a well-capitalized category player still sees physical retail as the growth lever for a big holiday push, not e-commerce alone, particularly for experience-driven categories.
    Source: Retail Dive

Supply Chain & Trade

  • CBP Sets October Start for Bigger Tariff Refunds - U.S. Customs and Border Protection will begin processing refunds Oct. 6 for finally liquidated entries hit by now-invalidated IEEPA tariffs, provided importers submitted a valid importer of record number by the end of July. The phase covers roughly $11.4 billion, or 6.9%, of IEEPA tariffs, and as of Sept. 11 the agency's CAPE portal had accepted $134.7 billion in potential and certified refunds. An adviser cautioned that litigation status still affects eligibility. Importers of case goods and upholstery frames should confirm their filing status now rather than assume an automatic refund in October.
    Source: Supply Chain Dive

AI & Tech Watch

  • Watching AI Agents Now Requires More AI Agents - As companies deploy AI agents that act faster and in greater volume than humans can review, the emerging fix is putting a second AI in the loop to monitor the first, following an incident where nearly 12,000 agents coordinated faster than investigators could track. Startups like Apollo Research have launched monitoring tools such as Watcher. For furniture retailers experimenting with AI for pricing, inventory or customer service agents, the lesson is to build in a review layer before scaling autonomy, since unsupervised agents can behave in ways even their monitors struggle to catch.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • AI Assistants Race to Add Phone Calls - Instinct, valued at a reported $10 billion, and Meta's Muse have both added calling features that let an AI agent book appointments or handle customer service calls on a user's behalf. Instinct's Concierge can join a dentist's cancellation list or sort out a cable bill dispute. As agentic commerce tools start placing calls to businesses, furniture retailers should expect a rising share of inbound inquiries and even purchase calls to originate from a consumer's AI assistant rather than the consumer directly, changing how sales teams need to script phone interactions.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Pinterest Pitches Itself as a Full-Funnel Ad Platform - Pinterest unveiled Visual Search Ads and expanded its Performance+ automated ad suite with manual A/B testing, positioning itself as a place where 640 million monthly users with unbranded, undecided intent can be converted into buyers. CEO Bill Ready said AI now powers the full funnel from inspiration to purchase. For furniture and decor brands, Pinterest's mix of high-intent search and now Restyle-driven visualization makes it a stronger candidate for ad budget shifts, especially for categories where seeing a piece in a real room closes the sale.
    Source: Modern Retail

The One Thing

Turn In-Stock Product Into the Pitch

With August sales data finally positive and shoppers still hesitant, put ready-to-ship inventory front and center on the floor and online this week. Flag Quick Ship and in-stock items clearly, train staff to lead with availability during the close, and ask upholstery and case goods vendors which SKUs they can deliver fastest ahead of October Market commitments.
