Wednesday, September 23, 2026

Good morning. High Point season is warming up early this year, with Kincaid, Flexsteel and UE Living all previewing new upholstery stories built around comfort science, warranties and safety rather than just fabric and finish. At the same time, the boardroom drama at Ethan Allen escalated with a definitive proxy filing from Doug Bergeron, and Natuzzi named a familiar face to steady its turnaround. Layer in fresh housing data, a Labor Day sales surprise and Amazon's standoff with Meta's shopping agent, and the throughline is clear: trust, whether in a warranty, a board or an AI checkout, is this week's currency.

Top Stories

1. Bergeron Files Definitive Proxy to Take On Ethan Allen's Board

Activist investor Doug Bergeron filed a definitive proxy statement with the SEC nominating five candidates, including himself, Anna Brockway, Steve Oblak, Kristine Miller and Stefanie Tsen Ward, for Ethan Allen's board, and has begun a pre-emptive CEO search. Bergeron's white-card slate is up against the incumbent board's blue card, led by Chairman and CEO Farooq Kathwari. Bergeron's letter to shareholders cites two decades of contraction, unresolved issues flagged by ISS back in 2015, and lagging growth versus RH, Williams-Sonoma, West Elm and Arhaus. Ethan Allen's own CEO search, announced Sept. 21, aims to name Kathwari's successor by June 30, 2027. For retailers watching a Top 100 peer, succession clarity and governance credibility are becoming as important to the brand's future as product.

Source: Furniture Today

2. Natuzzi Brings Back Aldo Uva to Lead a Turnaround

Natuzzi S.p.A. named Aldo Uva as its next CEO, effective within the first half of October 2026, returning him to a role he held from 2008 to 2009. Chairman Pasquale Natuzzi cited Uva's three decades of experience restoring growth and profitability at companies including Ferrero, Nestle and Indesit. Uva said the priority is simplifying the business, streamlining operations and reshaping the cost base while redirecting resources toward growth and innovation. Natuzzi distributes through 552 monobrand stores plus galleries and multi-brand placements worldwide. For retailers carrying the brand, the leadership change signals a push toward operational discipline that could reshape pricing, delivery commitments and marketing support in the year ahead.

Source: Furniture Today

3. Kincaid Upholstery Bets on Lifetime Warranties and a New Showroom

Nearing its one-year anniversary under KLM Furniture LLC ownership after the La-Z-Boy divestiture, Kincaid Upholstery is building a differentiation story around lifetime warranties on frames, springs and cushions, seven Worry-Free cushion configurations, and a photo-verification step that sends purchasers an image of custom orders before shipment. The company debuts a new 12,000-square-foot showroom at High Point Market sharing space with Valdese Weavers. Chief Commercial Officer Cole Mehlman said the warranties and direct support give sales associates and designers confidence to recommend the line without hesitation. Retailers selling upholstery on comfort alone can use this as a reminder that a documented guarantee closes hesitant shoppers as effectively as a lower price.

Source: Furniture Today

4. Flexsteel Rolls Out Its First New Spring in 20 Years

Flexsteel Inds. launched Comfort Choice, an upholstery system built around soft, balanced and supportive cushion tiers and the new SixFlex Blue Steel spring, only the sixth generation since 1934. Chief Growth Officer David Crimmins said a five-stage consumer research process found buyers will pay more for comfort and want customization, but prefer curated choice over open-ended options. Five new frames launch in more than 700 fabrics with no upgrade charge between comfort levels, targeting households earning $100,000-plus with sofas priced $1,699 to $2,199. Point-of-sale materials will match the showroom message exactly. Retailers get a ready-made comfort story and matching POS to train floor staff ahead of the full rollout at High Point Market.

Source: Furniture Today

5. UE Living Adds Anti-Pinch Sensing to Motion Upholstery

UE Living will debut SensoDrive, a motor that detects resistance during reclining and pauses motion to prevent pinching, as a standard feature across its motion upholstery line at High Point Market. General Manager Ron Yu said the technology preserves whisper-quiet, double-tap reclining while adding a safety layer. Manufacturing runs through UE's China and Vietnam operations, spanning modern, casual and transitional styles at multiple price points. The company will show the feature in its new fourth-floor showroom at Furniture Plaza, with a grand opening set for Oct. 16. For retailers fielding safety questions on power motion, a built-in sensing feature gives sales associates a concrete answer instead of a reassurance.

Industry Moves

  • Furniture Pioneer Paul Maitland-Smith Dies at 90 - Paul Maitland-Smith, who founded Maitland-Smith Ltd. in Hong Kong in 1979 and later Theodore Alexander in Vietnam in 1996, died Sept. 17 at 90. He pioneered luxury manufacturing across Southeast Asia, built an eclectic material vocabulary spanning bronze, shagreen and lost-wax brass casting, and grew Theodore Alexander into an operation employing roughly 5,000 people. He was also known for showroom hospitality and advocating higher wages and on-site health care for craftspeople.
    Source: Furniture Today
  • ISFD Names 107 Finalists for 30th Pinnacle Awards - The International Society of Furniture Designers named 107 finalists across 32 categories for its 30th annual Pinnacle Awards, spanning professional and student designers. Winners will be honored at a gala Oct. 19 during fall High Point Market. Executive Director David Blair said the finalists reflect three decades of shifting aesthetics, materials and manufacturing methods. Retailers can scan the finalist list for design cues likely to show up on showroom floors next year.
    Source: Furniture Today
  • Designer Buying Group Marks 10 Years, $9 Million in Orders - The Designers Collaborative, a 500-plus member buying group for interior designers, wrote $9 million in vendor orders last year and offers members up to 60% off MSRP from more than 300 vendors. Founder Heather McManus started the group after clients undercut her margins by finding products online. The group celebrates its 10th anniversary at October Market with events hosted by Massoud, IDS, Currey & Company and Sarreid. Retailers selling to designers should note that pooled buying power is reshaping who gets preferred pricing at market.
    Source: Furniture Today

Market Pulse

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • Labor Day Surprises Retailers With Trade-Up Demand - Retailers reported stronger than expected Labor Day results after flat Memorial Day and Fourth of July holidays, with strong motion sales and unexpected foot traffic. Povision reported sales up 300% year over year, with power sofas priced $2,500 to $4,000 leading. Jacksonville Bedding Mattresses & More finished up 141% with a simplified "save up to 50%" message, owner RJ Williams crediting phenomenal average tickets. Both retailers said customers chose premium and midrange products over deep discounts, a signal worth testing in fourth-quarter promotions.
    Source: Home News Now
  • Casper Reopens Flagship With Open, Education-First Layout - Casper reopened its redesigned NoHo flagship in New York with a more open layout showing eight mattresses on the main floor plus two private trial spaces for its Snow Max and Dream Max models and a new education wall. Director of Retail Douglas Atchison said the goal is giving shoppers space and guidance to buy confidently in person. Casper now operates more than 40 Sleep Shops and will reopen its Brooklyn City Point store in October with the same concept. Independent retailers can borrow the cue: fewer models shown better, plus trained staff, beats a crowded floor.
    Source: Furniture Today
  • Gen Z Shoppers Plan Short Furniture Ownership Cycles - New research sponsored by Bread Financial finds Gen Z shoppers plan to keep furniture just three to four years, timed to when they expect to move again. That short horizon points toward more frequent, smaller-ticket replacement purchases rather than heirloom buys. Retailers courting younger shoppers should consider financing terms and product durability messaging calibrated to a three-to-four-year ownership window rather than a decade-long sales pitch.
    Source: Home News Now

Supply Chain & Trade

  • Housing Completions Drop Sharply, Clouding Furniture Demand - August housing completions fell 27.1% year over year to 1,128,000 while starts declined 1.2% to 1,275,000, though building permits rose 3.5% to 1,394,000. Fewer completions mean fewer households moving in and furnishing homes, yet furniture store sales dropped just 2.7% year over year in July and held roughly level month over month, suggesting furniture demand is proving more resilient than new-home activity. Retailers should watch permit growth as a leading indicator while bracing for softer move-in-driven traffic through early next year.
    Source: Home News Now
  • Overseas Manufacturers Are Quietly Becoming the Retailer - A 26-year home theater seating veteran found that overseas manufacturers are bypassing U.S. importers and retailers entirely, registering U.S. shell companies, warehousing through third parties and selling direct on Amazon, Walmart and their own sites. Examples like Vorlux Seating and Weilianda Home show Chinese ownership behind seemingly American storefronts, with Marketplace Pulse pegging China-based sellers at 55.9% of Amazon's top 10,000 sellers. Independent retailers competing against pricing that looks domestic but isn't should ask vendors directly about ownership and where inventory risk actually sits before matching price.
    Source: Furniture Today

AI & Tech Watch

  • Amazon Pushes Back on Meta's Shopping Agent Muse - Amazon asked Meta to remove it from Muse, Meta's new AI purchasing agent, saying it never authorized access and was not given advance notice. Amazon argued third-party purchasing agents should operate openly and respect merchant opt-in, comparing the standard to delivery and travel booking apps. Amazon separately sued Perplexity's Comet agent last year over similar unauthorized access, a case still underway. For furniture sellers on marketplaces, agentic checkout is arriving faster than platform rules can keep up, making vendor terms of service worth a fresh read before any AI agent starts transacting on a retailer's behalf.
    Source: Retail Dive
  • Furniture.com Launches Shoppable Moodboards Across Brands - Furniture.com launched Moodboards, letting shoppers combine furniture from multiple retail partners with paint, wallpaper, flooring and fabric into one visual room plan with a single checkout. The company's research found shoppers visit more than four furniture brands and spend 19 days researching before buying. Co-founder Dan Bennett said the goal is better decisions, not more product choice. For an independent retailer whose online share sits in single digits, a cross-brand planning tool like this raises the bar on what a basic product page needs to offer to keep shoppers from finishing the decision elsewhere.
    Source: Furniture Today
  • Eye-Tracking Study Crowns Japandi the Most Captivating Style - UK retailer Sharps used eye-tracking technology on 150 participants viewing 25 global interior styles, finding Japandi scored highest at 88.7 out of 100 for grabbing and holding attention, ahead of Balinese and Tudor styles. Interiors expert Natalie Prince noted classic period styles like Tudor and Georgian outperformed modern movements like Bauhaus and Industrial. Merchandisers building vignettes or web imagery can use the finding as a data point for which room settings to feature first on a homepage or showroom entrance.
    Source: Furniture Today
  • Meta's Muse Agent Passes ChatGPT on App Store Charts - Muse, Meta's personal AI agent launched September 8, has passed ChatGPT on the App Store, according to a brief industry recruitment note. The rapid rise underscores how fast agentic shopping tools are gaining consumer adoption. Furniture retailers should treat AI agent traffic as a distribution channel to monitor now, not later, since an agent transacting on a shopper's behalf changes who controls the product description and price a customer actually sees.

The One Thing

Ask Vendors Who Carries the Inventory Risk

Between overseas manufacturers quietly becoming the retailer online and marketplaces racing to let AI agents buy without a merchant's consent, this week is a reminder that ownership and inventory risk are getting harder to see. Before matching a competitor's price or signing on with a new drop-ship partner, ask directly where the goods sit, who owns the brand, and what happens if an AI agent completes a sale nobody approved.
