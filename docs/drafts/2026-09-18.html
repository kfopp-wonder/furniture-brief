Good morning. Rowe Furniture is marking eight decades in business by bringing back an icon, Rooms To Go keeps stacking stores in Texas, and Jensen Outdoor is betting on inventory to protect its luxury lane heading into 2027. Meanwhile Bread Financial's Consumer Insights Now series turns a lens on Gen Z shoppers, a reminder that the next generation of furniture buyers has different constraints and different expectations. Add in Oriental Weavers finding upside in a slower housing market and Bob's Discount Furniture doubling down on creator-led video, and the theme is clear: growth right now comes from knowing the customer cold, not from waiting for the market to loosen up.

Top Stories

1. Rowe Marks 80 Years By Reissuing Gondola

Rowe Furniture is celebrating its 80th year in business by bringing back Gondola, a design that has kept a following among design enthusiasts long after its original run. The reissue leans on the piece's continued popularity rather than a full reinvention, tying the anniversary to a specific, recognizable product rather than a general marketing push. For a category where provenance and story sell as hard as fabric and frame, an anniversary built around one iconic piece gives dealers a concrete hook for floor talk and social content. Retailers carrying Rowe can use the reissue to reintroduce heritage design language to younger shoppers who never saw the original.

Source: Furniture Today

2. Rooms To Go Adds Conroe To Its Texas Buildout

Rooms To Go opened a new store in Conroe, a Houston suburb, continuing what Furniture Today describes as a broader expansion strategy across key Texas markets. The Top 100 retailer is treating Texas as a priority growth region rather than opening opportunistically. Sustained store growth from a chain of this scale signals confidence in Texas housing and population trends even as other markets stay cautious. Independent retailers in adjacent Texas trade areas should watch traffic and promotional intensity closely, since a well-capitalized competitor moving into a suburb changes the local price and financing conversation fast.

Source: Furniture Today

3. Jensen Outdoor Leans On Inventory For 2027 Confidence

Jensen Outdoor is extending its planning horizon and carrying additional inventory heading into 2027, betting that demand for high-end outdoor product will hold steady even as the broader housing market cools. The luxury outdoor supplier is choosing to absorb carrying costs now rather than risk being caught short if demand persists. That is a supplier volunteering to hold risk that could otherwise sit on a retailer's floor plan, which is exactly the posture dealers should reward with purchase commitments. Retailers in the luxury outdoor lane should ask suppliers directly whether they are extending lead times or building buffer stock for next year.

Source: Furniture Today

4. Oriental Weavers Bets On The Stay-Put Homeowner

Oriental Weavers USA is positioning itself around consumers who are staying in their current homes rather than moving, treating a slower housing market as an opening rather than a headwind. The rug supplier's read is that refresh spending, not relocation spending, is where the near-term dollars are. That reframes the sales conversation for retailers away from move-driven big-ticket replacement and toward layered updates that refresh a room without a full remodel. Floor teams can build vignettes and financing offers around rug and accent refreshes for customers who have decided not to move this year.

Source: Furniture Today

5. Bread Financial Data Puts Gen Z Needs Ahead Of Wants

Bread Financial's Consumer Insights Now series opened with Gen Z shoppers, ages 18 to 29, finding that limited funding pushes their furniture purchases toward needs rather than wants. Home News Now reports this as the first installment in a sponsored series tracking generational shopping behavior. For a generation entering first apartments and starter homes with tighter budgets, the practical implication is pricing and financing structure matter more than aspirational styling. Retailers courting younger buyers should lead with functional, entry-price assortments and promotional financing rather than premium storytelling that assumes discretionary income this cohort does not yet have.

Source: Home News Now

Industry Moves

  • ICFA Honors Rogers, Buzbee With Lifetime Achievement - The International Casual Furnishings Association gave its Lifetime Achievement Award to Terri Lee Rogers of OW Lee and Cathy Buzbee of Absco Fireplace & Patio at Casual Market Atlanta. Rogers has led OW Lee for nearly two decades, and Buzbee has grown her family's outdoor store into a multilocation business over 32 years. Joy Odom of Absco won the Dudley K. Flanders Leadership Award, and Kathy Jo Martin of Lloyd Flanders won the Mia Nault Unsung Hero Award. The recognitions underscore how much of the casual category still runs on multigenerational family leadership.
    Source: Casualnewsnow
  • FabricaKraft Builds A Business Around Custom Fit - Soft-furnishings brand FabricaKraft expects $1 million in revenue this year, built entirely on made-to-order outdoor cushions and furnishings rather than standard sizing. Co-founders Neha Khanna and Vibhas Sen, who launched the company in 2021, report a 60% to 70% conversion rate and a customer mix of roughly 75% retail and 25% B2B, including hospitality and Navy contracts. The company also uses fabric scraps from suppliers like Sunbrella. It is a reminder that in outdoor, precision fit and education on cost drivers can be a differentiator worth a premium.
    Source: Casualnewsnow
  • Casual Market Atlanta Wraps With A Chairman's Reception - The casual furnishings industry gathered for the ICFA Awards Gala during Casual Market Atlanta, with attendees mixing at a chairman's reception ahead of the awards program. The event underscores how much relationship-building around the outdoor category still happens face to face at market, alongside the product decisions. Retailers who did not attend can still use the honorees and award categories as a shorthand for which outdoor vendors and reps have staying power in a category built on service relationships as much as product.
    Source: Casualnewsnow

Market Pulse

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • Bob's Hands Its Video Series To Creators For Season 2 - Bob's Discount Furniture is launching season two of its social video series, which followed real couples redecorating a room and drove nearly $9 million in attributable sales from more than 152 million video views in season one. For season two, Bob's is handing creative control to established creator couples instead of managing production in-house. Chief Marketing Officer Stephen Nesle framed the shift as trading traditional advertising reach for authentic, creator-owned storytelling. Retailers without in-house content budgets can borrow the model at a smaller scale by partnering with local design-minded creators rather than producing polished ads.
    Source: Furniture Today
  • Amazon Raises Starting Wage As Spending Pressure Builds - Amazon is raising its core operations minimum starting wage by $1 to $20 per hour, pushing its average starting wage to about $24, and adding fee-free banking and grocery discount benefits for U.S. employees. The move comes as Navy Federal Credit Union's chief economist Heather Long warns of a likely consumer spending slowdown into early 2027 as real incomes flatten under gas and grocery inflation. For furniture retailers, wage inflation at the country's largest employer raises the cost floor for retail labor everywhere, while the spending warning reinforces why flexible financing terms matter more than ever this fall.
    Source: Retail Dive
  • Bread Financial Reads A Cash-Cautious Consumer - Mike Schmidt, a macro business analyst with Bread Financial, says consumers are treating cash as a precious commodity amid tariffs, high energy prices and other inflationary pressures, and that purchase power feels diminished compared to prior years. The reading lines up with Amazon's wage move and broader warnings of a slowdown ahead. For big-ticket furniture purchases, that means promotional financing and second-look credit options are doing more of the work of closing a sale than price alone. Retailers should revisit their financing waterfall now, before holiday traffic tests whether approval rates can keep up with more cautious shoppers.
    Source: Home News Now

AI & Tech Watch

  • Claude Merges Chat And Cowork Into One Window - Anthropic is combining Claude's chat interface and its Cowork workspace into a single experience, rolling out first to Pro and Max plans, so users no longer choose between tabs for quick questions versus bigger tasks. Claude now routes work automatically and retains context across a closed laptop or a mobile check-in. For a furniture business drowning in product data, spec sheets and vendor catalogs, a single AI interface that keeps context across a task lowers the barrier for merchandisers and marketing staff to actually use these tools daily instead of treating them as a novelty.
    Source: Claude
  • Claude Adds Docs And Slides Built From One Conversation - Alongside its interface merger, Claude is launching Docs and Slides features in beta, letting users generate a written report and a matching slide deck from the same conversation, then edit, comment and download as PowerPoint or PDF. Enterprise admins control rollout timing. For a furniture retailer's marketing or merchandising team, this collapses the step between pulling sales or inventory data and turning it into a vendor pitch or leadership update, cutting the manual work of rebuilding the same numbers in two formats.
    Source: Beehiiv
  • ElevenLabs Launches An AI Receptionist For Small Firms - ElevenLabs introduced an AI receptionist aimed at small businesses and freelancers, extending its voice AI work into front-line customer contact. Details are limited to the announcement itself, but the direction points at phone and chat triage being automated for smaller operators who cannot staff a dedicated line. Independent furniture stores fielding delivery-status calls and stock questions after hours could pilot a similar tool to capture leads and answer routine questions without adding headcount, freeing sales staff to focus on the floor.
    Source: Beehiiv
  • Anthropic Streamlines Claude Ahead Of A Crowded AI Fall - TechCrunch reports that Anthropic's interface merger addresses a real usability complaint, customers often did not know which Claude tab fit a given task, and folds Claude Design's website and prototype tools into the same window as chat and Cowork. The simplification comes as competing assistants push their own unified interfaces. For retailers evaluating AI tools for product descriptions, room visualizers or catalog work, the lesson is to test how much friction a platform removes before committing, since a confusing interface kills adoption on a busy sales floor faster than any feature gap.
    Source: TechCrunch

The One Thing

Match Your Financing Waterfall To A Cautious Buyer

Bread Financial's own analyst says consumers are guarding cash, and Amazon just raised wages while warning of a spending slowdown ahead. Before the fall selling season builds, retailers should stress-test their financing stack, promotional terms, second-look credit, and payment options, to make sure a nervous shopper still has a clear, affordable path to yes on a big-ticket purchase.
