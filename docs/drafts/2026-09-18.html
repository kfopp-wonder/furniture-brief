Friday, September 18, 2026

Good morning. Census data out this week shows furniture and home furnishings sales inching up 0.9% in August and 1.9% year over year, a modest gain that fits the cautious mood running through today's edition. Rooms To Go is doubling down on Texas with a new Conroe store, Rowe Furniture is turning its 80th anniversary into a provenance story with a 25-piece Gondola reissue, and Jensen Outdoor's president says affluent shoppers and strong supply chains are propping up 2027 plans. Meanwhile, the second installment of Consumer Insights Now lays out exactly how Gen Z shops, and what it will take to keep them buying in stores.

Top Stories

1. Furniture Store Sales Rise 1.9 Percent as Retail Rebounds

The U.S. Census Bureau's August Monthly Retail Trade Report showed total retail sales up 6% year over year to $773.9 billion, with furniture and home furnishings stores up 0.9% from July and 1.9% from August 2025. Retail expert Chip West attributes the furniture gain largely to baseline price adjustments and back-to-college move-in demand, and says July's dip now looks like a temporary blip rather than a trend. Furniture and home furnishings ranked among the weaker categories month over month, trailing non-store retailers, electronics and miscellaneous stores. West expects growth to continue through September and October as retailers push holiday inventory earlier, but warns sticky inflation and high energy costs are pushing shoppers toward store brands and delayed big-ticket purchases. Retailers should watch whether early holiday sell-in cannibalizes September traffic.

Source: Home News Now

2. Gen Z Reveals What It Wants From Furniture Shopping

Home News Now's Consumer Insights Now survey of 1,000 shoppers ages 18-29 finds this group expects to replace furniture in three to four years, tied to how often they move, and spent $500 to $999 on average across two purchases this year. Sofas, primary bedroom sets and occasional tables topped their purchase list, and 42% were replacing worn-out pieces while 36% wanted more comfort. Despite heavy online research, 68% of Gen Z shoppers did homework before buying, but a majority still purchased sofas, recliners and bedroom furniture in physical stores. The message for retailers: Gen Z is not abandoning showrooms, but comfort, style, easy assembly and mix-and-match flexibility need to be visible and easy to find on the floor.

Source: Home News Now

3. Rooms To Go Adds Its 34th Texas Store in Conroe

Top 100 retailer Rooms To Go opens a 50,000-square-foot store in Conroe on Sept. 26, its 34th Texas location since entering Dallas in 2000, with two more Houston-area stores under construction in Rosenberg and Baytown plus a San Marcos location near Austin. CEO Jeffrey Seaman told Furniture Today the company is deepening its footprint within markets it already serves and building larger-format stores as it updates interiors and merchandising. Seaman said he hopes to have eight Texas stores in development by the end of 2027. For competitors, the message is that a crowded, high-growth market like Texas still rewards scale and format investment rather than retreat.

Source: Bridgetowermedia

4. Rowe Turns 80 With a 25-Piece Gondola Reissue

Rowe Furniture is marking its 80th anniversary by reissuing its 1966 Gondola sofa, a design that now commands $7,000 to $9,000 in the vintage marketplace, as a limited edition of just 25 individually numbered and signed pieces debuting at High Point Market. President Bobby Robinson said the company wanted to honor the sofa's history while keeping originals distinct, offering two textile stories built around Kravet Couture and a wool-blend plaid. The sofa will not return to regular production. For retailers, the move shows how a heritage story, scarcity and craftsmanship provenance can justify premium pricing and market buzz without expanding SKU count.

Source: Bridgetowermedia

5. Jensen Outdoor Leans on Bolivia Sourcing and Special Orders

President Bryan Echols told Furniture Today that Jensen Outdoor's South American material sourcing, mainly from Bolivia, gives it proximity advantages even as periodic disruptions require planning buffers and extended purchasing horizons. Special orders have stayed strong even as early buying picked up, an unusual pattern Echols calls encouraging heading into 2027. Retailers are shifting more floor space to higher-end outdoor product as affluent shoppers keep spending, and Echols says suppliers must help partners build stronger in-store experiences and use accessories to raise tickets and close sales rather than discount. The lesson: sourcing resilience and premium merchandising are becoming inseparable strategies for outdoor specialists.

Source: Bridgetowermedia

Industry Moves

  • FabricaKraft Bets on Custom Fit Over Standard Sizes - Outdoor soft-furnishings brand FabricaKraft, founded in 2021 by Neha Khanna and Vibhas Sen, expects to hit $1 million in revenue this year with a made-to-order model built around project dimensions rather than off-the-shelf sizing. The company reports a 60% to 70% conversion rate, a 75% retail and 25% B2B mix, and plans to explore indoor furnishings next year. Retailers competing on off-the-shelf outdoor product might note how much conversion a true custom-fit offer can generate.
    Source: Casual News Now
  • ICFA Honors OW Lee and Absco Leaders at Atlanta Gala - The International Casual Furnishings Association named Terri Lee Rogers of OW Lee and Cathy Buzbee of Absco Fireplace & Patio as Lifetime Achievement honorees at its Casual Market Atlanta gala. Joy Odom of Absco won the Dudley K. Flanders Leadership Award and Kathy Jo Martin of Lloyd Flanders received the Mia Nault Unsung Hero Award. The awards underscore how long tenure and family continuity still define leadership credibility in the outdoor furnishings category.
    Source: Casual News Now
  • Nitori Shifts New China Stores Toward Kitchenware - IKEA rival Nitori is prioritizing kitchenware over furniture in new stores it opens in China, according to Nikkei Asia. The pivot signals that even a major home furnishings retailer sees stronger near-term demand in smaller, lower-ticket categories in a soft Chinese housing market. U.S. retailers watching consumer caution domestically might note that accessories and smaller-ticket categories can help sustain traffic when big-ticket furniture demand cools.
    Source: Nikkei Asia

Market Pulse

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • Oriental Weavers Bets on Rugs for the Home Refresh - With housing turnover slow, Oriental Weavers USA President Andy Brumlow sees rugs benefiting as consumers refresh homes they are not moving out of, launching the Kate and Keenan collections at New York Home Fashions Market with softer textures, muted palettes and updated traditional patterns. Brumlow said retailers should expect a value-conscious holiday season where design, price and dependable availability determine who wins. Merchants can lean on rugs as a low-investment upgrade to drive traffic when big-ticket furniture replacement slows.
    Source: Bridgetowermedia
  • Bread Financial Analyst Flags a More Cautious Consumer - Mike Schmidt, a macro business analyst with Bread Financial, said consumers increasingly treat cash as a precious commodity amid tariffs, high energy prices and inflation, eroding purchasing power even when spending holds up. For furniture retailers, that means finance conversations matter as much as product ones. A layered financing waterfall, pairing promotional financing with second-look and flexible-payment options, can convert hesitant shoppers who would otherwise walk away from a big-ticket purchase.
    Source: Home News Now
  • Amazon Raises Starting Wage as Consumer Pressure Builds - Amazon is raising its core minimum starting wage to $20 an hour, adding fee-free banking through First Tech Federal Credit Union and new grocery discounts, pushing its average starting wage to about $24. The move comes as Navy Federal's Chief Economist Heather Long warns of a likely consumer spending slowdown into early 2027 as inflation outpaces wage growth for many households. Furniture retailers should treat this as another signal that value messaging and financing flexibility, not just product, will carry holiday sales.
    Source: Retail Dive

Supply Chain & Trade

  • Streamline Art Leans Into Domestic Production for High Point - Streamline Art will debut more than 350 new products at High Point Market, including its domestically manufactured Woven Studio collection of 60 watercolor-style pieces printed on linen canvas in 14 sizes. President Mark Wiltshire said retailer requests for larger scale pieces drove the expansion, following strong results from April market. Domestic manufacturing gives Streamline Art shorter lead times and less exposure to tariff and freight volatility than fully imported art programs, a selling point retailers can raise with vendors still sourcing overseas.

The UN is now working with Google on a Data Commons platform to make its statistics more AI-accessible. Retailers relying on AI tools for market data or product claims should verify figures against a primary source before they land in a sales pitch or ad.
    Source: TechCrunch

The One Thing

Pair Every Big-Ticket Pitch With a Financing Option

Bread Financial's Mike Schmidt says consumers now treat cash as precious, and Amazon just raised wages while economists warn of a spending slowdown into 2027. Retailers should train floor staff this week to lead with financing options, not just price, on every sofa, mattress and bedroom sale, since a flexible payment path can convert a hesitant shopper who would otherwise walk.
