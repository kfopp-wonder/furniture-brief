Friday, September 18, 2026

Good morning. Census data confirms furniture and home furnishings sales are growing but trailing the broader retail rebound, a gap that shows up again in this week's Gen Z survey, where price and worn-out replacement drive most purchases even as three-quarters still buy sofas in-store. Rooms To Go is answering a tougher, bigger-footprint Texas market with a third new Houston-area store, while Rowe Furniture leans the opposite direction, turning an 80th anniversary into a 25-piece provenance story. Add in Legends Home's balancing act between domestic manufacturing and import sourcing, and the day adds up to a category stretching in two directions at once: volume and scarcity.

Top Stories

1. Furniture Sales Rise 1.9 Percent, Still Lagging Broader Retail

The U.S. Census Bureau's August retail report showed total sales up 6% year over year, while furniture and home furnishings store sales climbed a smaller 1.9% over August 2025 and 0.9% month over month. Retail expert Chip West attributed the gain to baseline price adjustments and back-to-college move-in demand, and furniture ranked among the weakest categories for the month alongside building materials and food and beverage stores. West expects overall retail spending to keep growing through October as retailers push holiday inventory earlier, even as shoppers lean on savings and store brands amid sticky inflation and high energy costs. Retailers should read the gap between overall retail strength and furniture's softer pace as a signal to sharpen promotions and financing offers rather than assume a broad consumer rebound will lift the category automatically.

Source: Home News Now

2. Gen Z Shoppers Buy on Price and Replace in Three Years

Home News Now's Consumer Insights Now survey of 1,000 consumers aged 18 to 29 found most spent $500 to $999 on two furniture items this year, with sofas, primary bedroom and occasional tables leading purchases. Some 42% were replacing worn-out furniture and 24% wanted fully assembled product, while 68% researched before buying and 19% used AI in their search. Despite digital research habits, about 72% still bought sofas in-store and 71% bought recliners in-store, confirming the showroom's continued role even for a generation shopping with short-term horizons. Retailers can use this data to frame financing and warranty conversations around durability expectations that top out at five years for over a third of these buyers, not lifetime investment.

Source: Home News Now

3. Rooms To Go Opens Conroe, Presses Texas Advantage

Top 100 retailer Rooms To Go opens a 50,000-square-foot store in Conroe on Sept. 26, its 34th Texas location and the first of three planned for the Houston market, with sites also under construction in Rosenberg, Baytown and San Marcos. CEO Jeffrey Seaman told Furniture Today the company is building larger footprints in its newer Texas stores and hopes to have eight more in development by the end of 2027. The expansion shows a Top 100 player doubling down on a competitive but growing market rather than retreating to safer territory. Independent retailers in adjacent markets should watch store design cues, since Rooms To Go treats footprint size as a merchandising tool, not just a real estate decision.

Source: Furniture Today

4. Rowe Turns 80 With a 25-Piece Gondola Reissue

Rowe Furniture will mark its 80th anniversary at High Point Market with a limited Anniversary Edition of its 1966 Gondola sofa, now commanding $7,000 to $9,000 in the vintage market, with only 25 units offered in two textile compositions and each piece signed, numbered and accompanied by a Certificate of Authenticity. President Bobby Robinson said the goal was to honor Gondola's history while keeping it distinct from surviving originals, and the sofa will not return to regular production. The move converts nostalgia and scarcity into a marketing story rather than a volume play. Retailers can borrow the tactic on a smaller scale: a numbered, limited run on a heritage item creates urgency and a press hook without disrupting core inventory.

Source: Furniture Today

5. Legends Home Balances Domestic Craft With Import Reach

A year into his role, Legends Home President David Reitz told Home News Now the company added 10 major accounts and found roughly $3 million in cost efficiencies by focusing on its strongest lines rather than chasing every opportunity. A Kaizen initiative with the Arizona Manufacturing Extension has generated an estimated $200,000 in savings, a 50% reduction in Work in Process and elimination of 4,000 operator steps. Reitz positioned Legends as one of the few remaining U.S. manufacturers able to pair domestic entertainment and mattress production with imported container business for bedroom and dining, adding investment in Vietnam and a new Asia director. The model gives retailers a hedge: a single vendor offering both quick domestic replenishment and broader import assortment reduces the number of supply relationships needed to fill a floor.

Source: Home News Now

Brumlow said holiday will favor companies balancing design, value and dependable availability as retailers stay deliberate with inventory. Retailers can position rugs as an easy upsell for customers who are not buying full room sets this season.

Market Pulse

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • Amazon Raises Starting Wage as Spending Slowdown Looms - Amazon is raising its core operations minimum starting wage to $20 per hour, pushing its average starting wage to about $24, and adding no-fee banking and grocery discount benefits for employees. The move comes as Navy Federal Credit Union's chief economist Heather Long warned a consumer spending slowdown is likely into early 2027 as gas and grocery prices squeeze middle and lower-income households. For furniture retailers, a wage hike at the country's largest employer raises the local labor cost bar and signals that big-ticket purchases will increasingly depend on financing rather than discretionary cash.
    Source: Retail Dive
  • Bread Financial Sees Consumers Guarding Cash Closely - Mike Schmidt, macro business analyst with Bread Financial, said consumers increasingly treat cash as a precious commodity amid economic uncertainty, with tariffs and high energy prices eroding purchasing power. That caution has direct implications for big-ticket categories like furniture, where financing determines whether a sale happens at all. Retailers should revisit promotional financing waterfalls, layering primary approval, second-look options and flexible payment plans so no qualified customer walks without a path to purchase, especially as holiday season approaches.
    Source: Home News Now
  • FabricaKraft Proves Custom Outdoor Can Scale Profitably - Soft-furnishings brand FabricaKraft expects to hit $1 million in revenue this year, co-founder Vibhas Sen told Casual News Now, built entirely on made-to-order sizing rather than standard inventory, with a 60% to 70% conversion rate and a customer mix of roughly 75% retail and 25% B2B. The company also uses spare Sunbrella fabric scraps to build sustainability into its pitch. The model shows special orders can be a growth engine rather than a fulfillment headache when paired with clear customer education on pricing. Retailers selling custom outdoor cushions can borrow the tactic of explaining cost drivers like L-shaped cuts upfront to protect conversion.
    Source: Casual News Now

The One Thing

Match Your Financing Waterfall to Cautious Buyers

Bread Financial's Mike Schmidt says consumers are guarding cash closely, and Census data confirms furniture sales are growing slower than retail overall. Before the holiday push, retailers should test their full financing stack, primary approval, second-look and flexible payment options, in one waterfall so a hesitant Gen Z or budget-conscious shopper always has a path to yes instead of walking out.
