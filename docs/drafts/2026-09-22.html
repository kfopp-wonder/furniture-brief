Good morning. Ethan Allen's board put a firm date on Farooq Kathwari's succession, a rare bit of public clarity from a company mid-proxy-fight, and it lands the same week Diamond Mattress commits to its own standalone High Point showroom, a signal that vendors keep betting on physical market presence even as e-commerce reshapes how furniture gets sold. Premarket sponsors weighed in on a choosier 2026 consumer, Costco widened its delivery marketplace footprint, and Amazon shut Meta's shopping agent out of its site, a skirmish that says plenty about who controls the checkout of the future.

Top Stories

1. Ethan Allen Sets a Hard Deadline for Its Next CEO

Ethan Allen's board opened a formal succession process and committed to naming a new CEO no later than June 30, 2027, when Chairman and CEO Farooq Kathwari's contract ends. Kathwari will stay on as a non-executive board member through the 2027 annual meeting, then step down. The Corporate Governance, Nominations and Sustainability Committee has hired a search firm to weigh internal and external candidates. The move comes amid an active proxy fight with activist investor Doug Bergeron, who is running his own parallel CEO search. For a Top 100 retailer built around Kathwari's four-decade tenure, the timeline itself is the news: it gives dealers, reps and suppliers a fixed point to plan around instead of an open-ended governance standoff.

Source: Furniture Today

2. Diamond Mattress Commits to Its First Standalone High Point Showroom

Diamond Mattress will open a dedicated showroom at IHFC, H-638, during October Market, moving out of a shared space with Fashion Bed Group/Sinca after debuting at High Point in 2024. More than 30 mattresses will be on display, including the re-engineered AutoPilot 2.0 smart sleep system, the Technogel luxury line and remerchandised Gel Grid and Black Diamond collections spanning roughly $2,199 to $4,999 at queen. CEO Shaun Pennington called it a chance to show retailers the full breadth of the portfolio. For dealers, a bigger, dedicated showroom means more SKUs to walk, more finance-friendly price tiers to test, and a clearer margin story, with Technogel alone offering retailer margins of 65 points or better.

Source: BedTimes Magazine

3. Amazon Locks Meta's Muse Shopping Agent Out of Its Site

Amazon began blocking Meta's AI assistant Muse from completing purchases on Amazon.com, citing a violation of its Conditions of Use for unauthorized AI agents. The move stops Muse users from buying goods on the platform through the agent. It underscores how retailers are drawing hard lines around who gets to transact on their behalf as agentic commerce tools multiply. For furniture retailers building or evaluating their own AI shopping tools, the lesson is that platform access is not guaranteed and vendor agreements will need explicit terms for agent-driven checkout, not just product data feeds.

Source: businessoffashion.com

4. Costco Adds DoorDash Nationwide, Deepens Uber Tie-Up

Costco expanded its Uber Eats partnership to nearly 600 U.S. locations and joined DoorDash's marketplace nationwide across roughly 630 stores in 47 states, building on relationships that already include Instacart. CEO Ron Vachris told investors in May that U.S. same-day delivery is growing faster than digital overall and is a strong loyalty driver among top-spending members. Uber is adding membership perks and fee waivers on orders over $60. For furniture retailers, Costco's multi-platform delivery buildout shows how a big-box competitor is normalizing fast fulfillment expectations; independents without a same-day or scheduled delivery option risk looking slow by comparison even on non-competing categories.

Source: Retail Dive

For furniture retailers whose online transaction share still lags the 30% to 40% range other categories report, this is a reason to test how discovery tools and financing offers actually surface to an AI-driven shopper, not just a human one browsing a website.

As home office remains a durable category post-pandemic, retailers can use the commercial-grade story, Blum glides, dovetail construction, multi-year warranties, to justify higher tickets against big-box alternatives.

His fix: keep a running sigh list of daily annoyances, then train an AI model on those tasks the way a manager onboards a new hire, with detailed prompts rather than vague requests. For independent retailers, the practical entry point is auditing repetitive tasks in inventory, CRM follow-up or listing descriptions before buying any new AI tool.

AI & Tech Watch

  • Amazon Shuts Out Meta's Muse Over Agentic Shopping Risk - Amazon began returning an error message blocking Meta's AI assistant Muse from completing purchases, stating that unauthorized AI agents violate its Conditions of Use. TechCrunch notes Amazon has its own foundation models and inference platform, and that a bad agent-driven order would leave Amazon handling both an angry customer and an angry vendor. For furniture sellers exploring marketplace listings, the episode is a preview of friction points once agentic buying scales: platforms will gate which agents can transact, not just which products get indexed.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Amazon-Meta Standoff Gets Wider Trade Coverage - Retail Gazette's coverage confirms Amazon has blocked Meta's new AI shopping agent from its website, adding to reporting that the two companies are drawing competing lines around agentic commerce access. The dispute reinforces that agent-driven shopping is arriving unevenly across platforms rather than as a single unified standard. Retailers evaluating AI shopping integrations should ask vendors specifically which platforms and agents are supported today, since access can change with little notice.
    Source: Retail Gazette
  • Meta's Muse Downloads Are Outpacing ChatGPT's Early Launch - Market intelligence firm Apptopia found Muse logged 1.8 million iOS downloads in the U.S. and Canada in its first 12 days versus 1.3 million for ChatGPT at the same stage, with daily active users also higher, 642,000 versus 231,000 overall. Muse has climbed to the No. 1 spot on the U.S. App Store. Fast consumer adoption of a general AI assistant matters for furniture marketers because any assistant with this kind of reach becomes a potential product-discovery channel, whether or not individual retailers are ready for it.
    Source: TechCrunch
  • Consumer Appetite for At-Home AI Tools Keeps Growing - A Jotform pulse check on everyday AI use found ordinary consumers increasingly want AI tools built into home life, and many still visit an original source even after getting an AI-generated answer. For furniture retailers, that behavior suggests product pages and room-planning content still earn traffic even in an AI-answer world, making it worth keeping detailed, accurate specs and dimensions on-site rather than assuming AI summaries will substitute for a real product page visit.
    Source: Jotform

The One Thing

Test Your Online Share This Week

With Costco widening delivery marketplaces and AI agents starting to fight over checkout access, independents should pull their own e-commerce transaction share and compare it against the 30% to 40% range other categories report. If online sits in single digits, the fastest fix is not a new website, it is adding one vendor's Quick Ship or drop-ship program so the floor can sell an endless aisle without carrying the inventory.
