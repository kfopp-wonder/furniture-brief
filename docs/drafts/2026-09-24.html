Good morning. At Home's furniture push shows up twice today, once in the product strategy and once in the loyalty program built around customer feedback, and together they describe a decor retailer trying to become a full-line competitor. Star Furniture is running its own experiment, putting general managers back on the selling floor with commission attached, and reporting double-digit gains in close rates. Furnitureland South and Avocado Green Mattress both leaned on brand partnerships to widen their reach, while a pair of AI surveys confirm that shoppers want help narrowing choices, not an algorithm with a credit card.

Top Stories

1. At Home Pushes Into Anchor Furniture With Fall Collections

At Home rolled out four new fall furniture collections spanning sofas, sectionals, dining tables and more substantial casegoods, part of a deliberate shift toward what VP of merchandise Chris Callahan calls a luxe for less, whole-home strategy. The retailer upgraded upholstery, stain-resistant fabrics, solid woods and finishes, and is rolling out three to seven merchandised vignettes per store to show shoppers full-room solutions rather than isolated accent pieces. For a Top 100 chain long known for decor, the pivot to anchor furniture is a direct bid for wallet share against traditional furniture retailers. Independents should expect a decor player showing up in furniture searches and floor comparisons that used to skip them entirely.

Source: Furniture Today

2. Furnitureland South Opens Dedicated Margaritaville Gallery

Furnitureland South cut the ribbon on an 8,500 square foot Margaritaville gallery on Sept. 21, built alongside its existing Hooker Furnishings spaces and produced under Hooker's license for the brand. CEO Jeff Harris and Hooker CEO Jeremy Hoff both framed the space as a lifestyle destination, with coastal, desert and mountain-themed vignettes meant to show the collection's versatility beyond a beach cliche. Licensed lifestyle brands give retailers a built-in marketing engine and a reason for repeat visits. Retailers weighing gallery space should note the payoff came from full-room styling, not just placing branded SKUs on a showroom floor.

Source: Furniture Today

3. Star Furniture Puts Commissioned Managers Back on the Floor

Star Furniture, part of Berkshire Hathaway's furniture division, has expanded its selling general manager model chainwide after a one-year pilot, shifting GMs from straight salary to a smaller salary plus commission and putting them on the selling floor. VP of stores Rodney Watson said close rates rose 15% to 20% depending on the store, with average ticket and financing attachment also climbing. The change erased what Watson called the canyon between management and sales staff and created a clearer career path for top performers. Independents with flat management structures have a live case study for tying leadership pay to floor performance without triggering turnover.

Source: Furniture Today

4. Avocado Green Mattress Adds 24 City Home Doors in Florida

Avocado Green Mattress placed its organic mattress and sleep accessory lineup in 24 of City Home's 25 Florida locations, the latest brick-and-mortar expansion after adding Living Spaces stores and Kansas City's Furniture Mall this year. City Home VP of bedding Kevin Split tied the partnership to the retailer's 2040 Green Promise sustainability push. The deal gives Avocado physical touch points where shoppers can try before buying, while City Home gains a certified organic bedding line that fits its brand story. Retailers courting sustainability-minded bedding shoppers can use this as a template for a differentiated vendor conversation.

Source: Furniture Today

5. HS2 Widens Spring Portfolio Beyond Microcoils for North America

HS2, the 13-year joint venture between Hickory Springs Manufacturing and England's Spinks, is expanding beyond its established microcoil business into individually wrapped coils, open-coil systems and other spring components for North American mattress makers. Many of the new coil concepts are patented or patent-pending, and HS2 will offer customer exclusivity tied to volume commitments so manufacturers can build differentiated collections. President Tim Becker and CCO Darren Marcangelo both framed the move as domestic manufacturing muscle paired with design innovation. Mattress manufacturers weighing private label or exclusive constructions now have a broader domestic sourcing option to negotiate against overseas suppliers.

Source: BedTimes Magazine

Global Retail was the bright spot, with sales up 2.6% and North America orders up 7.5%, while both contract segments declined. Dedicated galleries give heritage brands like Stickley a controlled presentation and give the host retailer a differentiated draw that's harder for big-box and online sellers to replicate on price alone.

Market Pulse

Retail & Consumer Trends

  • At Home Rebuilds Loyalty Program Around Customer Research - At Home replaced its Insider Perks program with Design Rewards, offering a $10 coupon for every 1,000 points, four points per dollar for baseline members and five points per dollar for VIPs spending $350 or more annually. Head of loyalty David Coulson said focus groups, CRM testing and a conjoint study shaped the redesign, and the retailer plans an ongoing customer panel to keep refining it. Retailers rethinking loyalty perks have a model for treating the program as a continuous listening exercise, not a one-time launch.
    Source: Retail Dive
  • Target Puts Design Story at Center of New Brand Campaign - Target launched Inside Out, a national campaign running through Oct. 31 that centers customers and its red shopping bags, arriving as its turnaround shows early traction with Q2 comparable sales up 3.8%. New chief marketing and guest experience officer Mark Weinstein, formerly of Hilton, is steering the design-forward pivot. For home furnishings sellers, it's a reminder that design credibility is becoming a marketing lever even for mass retailers, raising the bar for how independents present their own room stories.
    Source: Retail Dive
  • At Home's Decor-to-Furniture Pivot Draws Trade Press Attention - Home Accents Today flagged At Home's expansion from decor into whole-home retail, echoing the furniture assortment overhaul detailed elsewhere today. The attention signals that competitors and suppliers are watching how a decor-first chain repositions itself as a furniture destination. Retailers should watch whether At Home's vignette merchandising and price points pull share from mid-market furniture stores or simply grow the decor customer's basket.
    Source: homeaccentstoday.com

AI & Tech Watch

  • Shoppers Want AI's Advice, Not Its Purchasing Power - Only 7% of fashion shoppers would let an AI assistant buy without approval, and 53% said they're uncomfortable with AI purchasing on their behalf, according to an ACI Worldwide survey of more than 3,300 U.S. and U.K. consumers. Shoppers valued price alerts and cross-retailer comparisons most. Marie Driscoll of the Retail Marketing Society said consumers want AI to narrow options, not make the final call. Furniture retailers building AI tools should design them as curated shortlists that hand the close back to a person or a checkout the shopper controls.
    Source: Retail Dive
  • A Third of Shoppers Regret an AI-Driven Purchase - A SmartCustomer survey of nearly 1,200 U.S. consumers found 89% don't fully trust AI recommendations, yet 76% used AI to shop in the past year, and a third made a regrettable purchase based on one. Apparel and footwear accounted for 45% of bad calls. More than half suspect companies feed AI misleading information to influence recommendations. Furniture sellers using AI-generated product descriptions or recommendations should audit accuracy closely, since a bad AI-driven mattress or sofa recommendation is a costly return, not a cheap one.
    Source: Retail Dive
  • Meta Tests Human Backup for Its Personal AI Agent - Meta is testing a human concierge layer for its new personal AI agent, according to Inside Retail, suggesting even platform giants see limits to fully autonomous AI shopping. The excerpt gives no further detail on scope or timing. For furniture retailers, it reinforces that the near-term winning model pairs AI research tools with a human who can close, matching what shoppers themselves say they want in the two surveys above.
    Source: Insideretail
  • AI-Native Startup Investing Draws Venture Capital Attention - Alumni Ventures is marketing a syndicate and fund structure built around startups that treat artificial intelligence as core infrastructure, offering accredited investors co-investment access alongside firms like Andreessen Horowitz and Sequoia. The pitch itself isn't retail-specific, but it signals continued capital flow into AI tooling that will eventually surface in retail product search, visual merchandising and agentic commerce. Furniture retailers should expect more vendor pitches built on freshly funded AI platforms and should vet each one against a concrete sales-floor or product-data problem, not the funding round behind it.
    Source: Av

The One Thing

Put a Commission Behind Your Best Coaches

Star Furniture moved general managers from flat salary to salary plus commission and put them back on the floor, and close rates rose 15% to 20%. Retailers with underused management talent can pilot the same shift in one store this quarter: give a top manager a commission stake, track close rate and average ticket for 60 days, and decide from real numbers rather than instinct.
